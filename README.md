# Crafting Connections II
## Continuing to Build Wellbeing and Community in the PhD Journey and Beyond 

People working at universities face high psychosocial risks. PhD students are particularly vulnerable. We propose this workshop,
focusing on using craft for fostering wellbeing and community connection, would be beneficial because everyone can share how
they approach wellbeing in their workspaces, and experience first hand how crafting activities have positively impacted the work
environment of the organisers’ reading group. Workshop attendees will get to experience crafting activities that are meaningful for
the workshop organisers; and contribute to a discussion on strategies to support wellbeing of university staff and research students
Australia-wide.

## Workshop and Activities
In this workshop, we will explore the connection between crafting and wellbeing through hands-on experience with
crafting activities. Participants will discuss and reflect on their experiences in academia, including the HDR journey,
and how they perceive wellbeing for themselves and their colleagues.
During the workshop, we will repeat our informal recording of the experience through post-it notes, but also more
formally record the reflections at the end. As last year, we would welcome the opportunity to display some of the crafts
after the workshop, and would be open to any opportunities to continue hosting crafts in a "pop-up" format throughout
the conference.
We intend to gather information from workshop participants about how the OzCHI community supports wellbeing.
After the workshop, the organisers will write a workshop report collating different wellbeing approaches that have
worked in labs to highlight best practices, and provide recommendations for incorporating crafting activities into
university support for students and staff ongoing. 

## Attending
We welcome participants of all academic levels who like the idea of meeting people while making beautiful things, and
who work in diverse teams and want to try new ways to include others and connect.

## Agenda

This is a half day workshop structured around crafting and self reflection. On the day participants will take part in two
different crafts and a reflection task. The day is structured as follows:
- 10 mins - Workshop Start and Introduction by Hosts
- 10 mins - Icebreaker task - What does wellbeing mean to me?
- 60 mins - Craft Session 1 - choose a table
- 30 mins - Break and switch
- 60 mins - Craft Session 2 - choose a different table
- 60 mins - Reflection task - Final discussion on the implications of crafting in HCI
- 10 mins - Wrap up and Thanks
  
Each participant will get to experience 2 of the 4 craft activities on offer. Participants may be directed to specific activities based on availability.

## Activities
### European Loom Knitting facilitated by Alexandra Douglass-Bonner. 

Knitting using looms (or Lucets) dates back to the 10th Century.
Due to their small size and simplicity, knitting looms can make knitting more accessible. Alex’s grandma taught her to
knit cords on a loom around the age of 3, and she came back to loom knitting years later to make hats for her friends.
Knitting has been shown to have positive effects on wellbeing and has been researched as a way to foster community
[18]. During the workshop she will show you a portable process to make cords using simple stitches that make up all
garments, building connections and fostering discussions on a wide range of topics. The cords produced can be used as
visual indicators for energy levels [23], which has been a valuable tool within her home.
 
### Zine-Making facilitated by Agata Filiana. 
 
A zine, or self-published magazine, has long been used as a tool to express diverse
opinions, such as voicing against oppressive systems and exploring emotions. In her research, Agata uses
zine-making with a simple template as a way to explore spirituality and wellbeing, using drawings and
stamps. In the workshop, we will explore what wellbeing means for HCI researchers. This session allows us to make
meaning of our research journey and create a space to share and connect with other HCI researchers. Materials such as
paper, pens, and old magazines will be provided, but we invite attendees to bring their own keepsakes (such as photos,
notes or other paper trails) that may be useful to the zine-making process.
 
### Bookmark Making facilitated by Dana Trenaman. 

Pattern-making, papercraft and abstract drawing are creative practices
that can be done almost anywhere with whatever materials are available. Dana began doodling patterns in the margins
of her school books in high school, and over time this evolved into a creative outlet, a form of self-care and a way to
connect with others. What she loves most about this craft is that there is no set design to follow and no mistakes
to make, with each piece evolving through exploration and creativity. As a mum of four, she values crafts that are
portable, affordable and easy to share with others. For this workshop, she will guide participants in creating personalised
bookmarks, a small and practical project that can be taken home and used every day. She hopes to encourage creativity,
connection and conversation while sharing a craft that people of all ages and skill levels can make their own.
 
### Painting facilitated by Jessica Korte. 
Painting is arguably the prototypical art form for English speakers - it certainly is in
Jessica’s mind. Jessica paints canvases and miniatures with family and friends as a way of building connection. She
invites workshop attendees to join her for freestyle or guided art to express themselves through colour. 
(Note: Painting will use acrylic markers rather than paint and water, to reduce risk of spills.)
